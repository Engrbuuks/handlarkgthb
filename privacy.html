Who we are

Handlark is a task manager for business owners, agencies and the remote assistants and clients they work with. It is run by Handlark ("we", "us"). You can reach us about privacy at hello@example.com.

This policy explains what personal data we handle when you use the website, the app, client pages and our emails, why we handle it, where it is kept, who helps us run the service, how long we keep it and the rights you have.

It is written for Nigeria's Data Protection Act 2023 (NDPA) and, for people in the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom, the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR and UK GDPR).

Our role and your workspace owner's role

Each workspace belongs to its owner, usually a business or agency. For the content people put in a workspace, such as tasks, comments, files, clients, time records, payroll figures and invoices, the business that owns the workspace decides why and how that data is used. Under the law the workspace owner is the controller of that content and we are its processor: we store and process it only to run the service for them, on their instructions, and under these terms.

We are the controller for account details, billing records, security logs and the data we use to run, protect and improve the service.

If you are an assistant, a staff member or a client of a business that uses Handlark, questions about how that business uses your information are best sent to that business first. We will help them answer.

Some features are private to each person by design: the reasons someone gives for feeling stuck, their mood answers, their own time estimates and their personal patterns are visible only to that person, never to managers. Tasks marked private are hidden from others and are never sent to the AI.

Time assurance is kind by design. There is no keystroke logging and no webcam, and no screenshots unless the workspace owner turns on Proof mode for a client and you agree to it (see Proof mode screenshots). Otherwise it only reads the time, updates and files people already add. Who sees what: each person sees the flags, checks and claims about their own time, with a plain explanation; the workspace owner sees them for the team, along with the budget vs actual report, which compares time with the budget the owner or the task's creator set and never with anyone's own estimate; other members see only their own row of that report and the team medians. Managers and team leads see the time, flags, leave and attendance of the people they look after. Everyone else sees only that a colleague is on approved leave and when, never the type of leave or its note, and the AI summary is told only who is away. Managers and team leads see these never anyone's private tasks, never people the owner hid, and never Proof mode screenshots; they see rates, pay and billing only if the owner allows a manager to. With planned shifts, each person is told about a late start, a missed shift or an early finish first and can give a reason; their team lead hears only if nothing is explained in time. A client sees only billable, confirmed time on their own page, never a hidden person's name, and never notes kept inside the team.

Please do not put special categories of data, such as health details, into tasks unless your business has a lawful reason and has told the people concerned.

Proof mode screenshots

Proof mode is off unless a workspace owner switches it on for one of their clients. When it is on, it works like this:

  • What a screenshot contains. A picture of the screen, window or browser tab you choose to share, at most 1280 pixels wide, blurred so text cannot be read unless the owner chose sharp pictures for that client (the consent screen says which). It can show anything that is on that screen at that moment, which may include personal data about you or other people, such as messages, names or account details. Share a single window, close what is private, or use Pause proof for 10 minutes first. With each screenshot we keep who took it, the client, task and time entry, the time, whether it was blurred, what kind of surface was shared (entire screen, window or tab) and a small number saying how much the picture changed since the last one. We never record keystrokes, mouse clicks, the names of apps or websites, your camera or your microphone.
  • When. Only on a computer, in Chrome, Edge or Firefox, only while you share your screen in that browser and a timer or client clock runs for that client: 1 to 3 at random times in each 10 minutes, as the owner chose. Phones cannot share a screen, so nothing is captured on a phone.
  • Purpose. To give the workspace owner, and if they choose a single client, evidence that time billed to that client was spent on that work. Not for any other purpose, and never for advertising or to train AI.
  • Lawful basis. Your consent. Only you can give it, on the consent screen, for each client, and it is recorded with the time and the version of the text you read. The owner cannot consent for you. You can withdraw it at any time in your account: nothing more is captured, and your screenshots for that client are deleted, except ones on time that was already approved or invoiced: those stay as the evidence for that time until the usual retention period ends. You then cannot add time for that client until you agree again or the owner switches Proof mode off for you. The workspace owner decides to use Proof mode and is responsible for having a good reason and for telling you about it; for these screenshots they are the controller and we process them for them.
  • Who sees them. You first: each screenshot waits in your Proof tray for 10 minutes, where you can delete it. A deleted one leaves only a marker that one was removed, never the picture. After that the workspace owner can see it. Other members never see your screenshots. The client never sees them, unless the owner chooses to show a particular screenshot on a line of time on that client's page, which is recorded in the workspace history.
  • How long they are kept. 30 days by default; the owner can choose 7, 14, 30 or 60 days. Then the hourly job deletes the screenshot and its file. They are also deleted when you leave or are removed from the workspace, those for a client when you withdraw your consent for it (except ones on time already approved or invoiced, which stay until their retention period ends), and with the workspace when it is deleted. Exports contain their details only, unless the owner chooses to include the pictures in a full export.
  • Where. In the same private file storage as other workspace files, under that workspace, reached only through links that last one hour.

Insights and the idle check

Insights help a team plan its work and look after its people. They are built only from what Handlark already records: time entries, the time clock, focus sessions, meetings, planned shifts, leave, time budgets and review flags. There is no app or website tracking, no keystroke, mouse or activity score, no microphone or calendar reading, and nobody is ever ranked against anyone else.

  • You see yours first. Under My patterns each person sees their own focus and meeting time, utilization, capacity, unusual time and wellbeing signals, with a gentle suggestion only they see. A weekly note about last week can come on Monday morning, held until your working day starts; you can switch it off.
  • What leads see. The workspace owner, managers and team leads see, for the people they look after, hours tracked, billable hours, hours available, utilization, capacity ahead (meetings and the time budgets of work due) and the unusual time report, which they can mark reviewed with a note. Focus and meeting time reach them only as one total for everyone they look after, when that is 3 or more people, rounded to the hour, counting each person only for the time they were among the people that lead looks after, and never for one team they pick (two teams side by side could give away one person's time). Your own estimates, private tasks and private lists never count toward what they see.
  • Sharing focus totals. The workspace owner may choose Share focus and meeting totals with leads, which is off by default. Then your totals show to them only after you have opened your own My patterns since it was switched on.
  • Wellbeing signals stay yours. A lead sees only a count of a signal shared by 2 or more people among everyone they look after, nothing when that is fewer than 3 people and nothing for one team they pick, never who. You can choose Share my signals with my lead, and stop at any time; the history records when you do.
  • The idle check. Off unless the workspace owner turns it on. It works only inside the Handlark tab, never in other apps or tabs and never at the operating system level. While a timer runs, the tab notes the moment of your last key press, click, tap or scroll there, but not what it was; if the tab has been quiet for the minutes the owner chose, the usual “Still working?” check is asked sooner, and only that moment is sent. It applies to you only after you have read what it does, and if you choose I often work in other apps, a quiet tab is never counted against you; the usual check (and Proof mode, if your owner uses it for a client) still applies. Nothing is decided automatically: an unanswered check sets the time aside for you to claim, as described above.
  • AI summaries. If the owner asks for it, the AI daily summary can include team totals from insights for the last 7 days: hours, billable time, focus and meetings as team totals, capacity, attendance and the number of unusual time flags, and wellbeing only as team counts. Never a person's wellbeing, never a client summary, and per person focus only when the owner shares it.

The browser extension

The Handlark extension for Chrome, Edge and Firefox is optional. It starts and stops your timer, shows today's tasks and lets you add a task, using the same public API and rules as the app.

  • Signing in. You connect it with a code shown in the app, which works once for 10 minutes, or with a personal API token you made with only tasks, time and clients ticked; a token that can do more is refused. Before it is used, the extension shows the workspace and the name it belongs to and asks you to confirm, so a code someone else sent you cannot quietly connect you to their workspace; until you do, the connection does nothing, and one you never confirm is cancelled after 10 minutes. The token can read and change only your tasks and time and read client names; it can never see invoices, pay or other people's details. It is kept encrypted in the extension's own storage on your computer, where the websites it adds buttons to cannot read it. The app lists each connected browser with its computer (such as Chrome on Windows), and you can disconnect it from the extension or the app at any time.
  • Sites. It asks for each site separately (GitHub, Jira, Trello, Asana, Google Docs, Gmail), only when you switch it on. On those pages it reads only the title and web address, and only sends them to us when you press Start timer, to name and link the task.
  • What it never does. It never captures your screen, never records keys, clicks or the sites you visit, and has no analytics. Proof mode still needs the web app and your consent there.
  • Idle hint, only if you turn it on. The browser tells the extension when your computer has been idle; if a timer is running, the extension asks whether to keep or remove the time away. Only your answer and the time it applies to are sent, as with the still working check.
  • Stores. When you install it from the Chrome Web Store, Microsoft Edge Add ons or Firefox Add ons, those stores handle the download under their own privacy policies. We receive nothing from them about you.

Paying staff through the agency's own Paystack

A workspace owner can pay their people from their own Paystack account. We never hold, receive or move that money, and we do not charge for it. When it is used:

  • What goes to Paystack. To check an account and pay it, we send the owner's Paystack account your name on the account, bank or mobile money code, account number, currency, the amount and a reference. Paystack is then a processor for the workspace owner, under Paystack's own terms and privacy policy, and keeps its own records of the transfer.
  • What we keep. The name Paystack confirmed, the bank name, the last 4 digits, the encrypted account number and recipient code, and for each payment the amount, currency, reference, status and the encrypted recipient code it was sent to. Never a full account number in the history, an export, the API or an email.
  • Who sees it. You see your own payslips in your account and get each one by email. The owner and managers allowed to handle pay see amounts and statuses for the people they look after. Clients never see anything about pay.
  • Safety. Sending pay always needs a fresh code from the sender's authenticator app, and changes to your details are emailed to you. Amounts are worked out on our server from the records, never taken from a browser. Payout details changed by anyone but you wait for you, or the owner, to confirm them before anyone is paid to them, and you get an email and a note in your account. A manager who changes your pay is reported to the owner, and a large rise, a new pay type or currency, or overtime rules that would pay you more wait for the owner to approve them. Nobody but the owner approves or sends their own pay. Details someone other than you changed in the last 7 days, the sender included, need the sender to confirm they checked them with you, and a transfer tried again always goes to the account it was first made for.
  • Invoice reminders. If the owner turns them on, a client gets a friendly email about an unpaid invoice on the days the owner chose, with a one click unsubscribe.

Where data is stored and transfers

Our database is hosted in the United Kingdom and some providers above process data in the United States, the European Union and other countries. When personal data leaves Nigeria, the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom, we rely on an adequacy decision where one exists, or on the provider's standard contractual clauses and the other safeguards the NDPA and GDPR allow.

How long we keep it

  • Workspace content stays while the workspace exists. When its owner deletes a workspace, everyone loses access at once. The owner can restore it for 30 days; after that its content and files are purged from our systems.
  • Deleted tasks go to a bin for 30 days so they can be restored, then they and their files are removed.
  • Proof mode screenshots are kept for the period the workspace owner chose (7, 14, 30 or 60 days; 30 by default), then deleted with their files.
  • The history of changes in a workspace and the record of requests the app has already applied are kept for 400 days, then deleted. Copies of the emails we sent are kept for 30 days.
  • The webhook and chat delivery log is kept for 30 days, then deleted. API tokens are kept until they are revoked or their person leaves the workspace. The record of who each lead or manager looked after, and when, is kept for 200 days. Revoked API tokens are deleted 30 days after they are revoked. Webhooks and chat alerts are kept until they are removed, or the person who added them leaves. Payout details are kept until you or the owner remove them, or you leave the workspace. Approved pay runs and payout batches (amounts, statuses, the bank and last 4 digits) are financial records: approved pay runs and payout batches are kept for 7 years, then deleted.
  • Attendance notes (whether a planned shift was late, missed or left early, when the person and their lead were told, and the reason given) and shift swap requests are kept for 400 days, then deleted. Planned shifts, roles, teams and leave balances are workspace content and are kept with it.
  • Insights are worked out when someone looks and are not stored. A lead's review of a week of unusual time, with its note, and your own insight choices are workspace content and are kept with it. Idle checks are still working checks and are kept the same way.
  • Answers to the “still working?” check are kept for 120 days, then deleted. Time entries, with their flags, claims, the owner's notes and the client's approvals and questions, are workspace content and are kept with it.
  • Usage counters used for rate limits are kept for 2 days, and monthly counts of WhatsApp messages sent are kept for 13 months.
  • When you delete your account, your sign in is removed and your name is removed from workspaces you do not own. Work you did in a workspace that belongs to someone else stays with that workspace, because it belongs to its owner.
  • Payment notices from Paystack and Lemon Squeezy are kept as a short record (type, amount, currency, reference, status and time), never the full notice, and deleted after 13 months. Receipts for your Handlark plan are issued and kept by Paystack or Lemon Squeezy under their own tax duties. Invoices an agency sends its clients are workspace content: they stay while the workspace exists and are purged with it, so export them first if you must keep them for tax. Time already put on an invoice is archived after 400 days but still kept with the workspace.
  • Codes for connecting the browser extension last 10 minutes and are deleted within the hour if not used. The record of an accepted data processing agreement is kept while the workspace exists.
  • Backups: we keep an encrypted backup of the database every night for 35 days and one a month for 13 months, and an encrypted weekly copy of attached files, stored apart from the live service with a key we keep offline. They are used only to recover the service after a fault or an accident. When a workspace is deleted, it is purged from the live service after its 30 day restore window; deleted files leave the file backups within 35 days, and the database backups that still hold it are deleted as they age out, within 13 months. If we ever have to restore a backup, we delete again anything that had already been deleted.

Your rights

Under the NDPA and the GDPR you have the right to:

  • be told how your data is used (this policy),
  • get a copy of your data. Owners can export the whole workspace at any time, each member can export everything they can see in it, and anyone can ask us for a copy of what we hold,
  • have wrong data corrected, most of which you can do yourself in your account,
  • have your data deleted. You can delete your account from your account in the app or by writing to us, and owners can delete a workspace,
  • restrict or object to some uses, including any use based on legitimate interests,
  • receive your data in a common machine readable format and have it sent to another provider,
  • withdraw consent at any time, for example by turning off location, phone alerts or WhatsApp alerts,
  • not be subject to decisions made only by automated means that have legal or similarly significant effects. We do not make such decisions,
  • complain to a regulator: the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (ndpc.gov.ng), or your local data protection authority in the EEA or the Information Commissioner's Office in the United Kingdom.

To use any of these rights, write to hello@example.com. We will answer within 30 days. We may need to confirm who you are first. If your request is about a workspace that belongs to a business, we may pass it to that business, as it controls that content.

Cookies and local storage

Handlark does not use advertising or tracking cookies, and there are no analytics trackers on the site.

The app keeps a few things in your browser's local storage so it works: your sign in session, the workspace you last opened, changes waiting to be sent when you are offline, alert settings, a referral code if you arrived through one, and on client pages the name you typed. If you pick a language, that choice is kept too. The browser extension keeps its own token (encrypted, under a key the browser keeps in the extension's own storage), server address, site choices and idle hint setting in the extension's storage on your computer. Phones may keep a copy of the app's screens so it opens offline. Clearing your browser data removes all of this.

Cloudflare may set a strictly necessary security cookie to protect the site from abuse.

How we protect data

Data travels encrypted, is stored encrypted, and each workspace is locked to its own members. Backups are encrypted before they are stored, with a key we keep offline. Our security page explains the measures in plain words.
