Who we are

Handlark is a task manager for business owners, agencies and the remote assistants and clients they work with. It is run by Handlark ("we", "us"). You can reach us about privacy at hello@example.com.

This policy explains what personal data we handle when you use the website, the app, client pages and our emails, why we handle it, where it is kept, who helps us run the service, how long we keep it and the rights you have.

It is written for Nigeria's Data Protection Act 2023 (NDPA) and, for people in the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom, the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR and UK GDPR).

Our role and your workspace owner's role

Each workspace belongs to its owner, usually a business or agency. For the content people put in a workspace, such as tasks, comments, files, clients, time records, payroll figures and invoices, the business that owns the workspace decides why and how that data is used. Under the law the workspace owner is the controller of that content and we are its processor: we store and process it only to run the service for them, on their instructions, and under these terms.

We are the controller for account details, billing records, security logs and the data we use to run, protect and improve the service.

If you are an assistant, a staff member or a client of a business that uses Handlark, questions about how that business uses your information are best sent to that business first. We will help them answer.

Some features are private to each person by design: the reasons someone gives for feeling stuck, their mood answers, their own time estimates and their personal patterns are visible only to that person, never to managers. Tasks marked private are hidden from others and are never sent to the AI.

Time assurance is kind by design. There is no keystroke logging and no webcam, and no screenshots unless the workspace owner turns on Proof mode for a client and you agree to it (see Proof mode screenshots). Otherwise it only reads the time, updates and files people already add. Who sees what: each person sees the flags, checks and claims about their own time, with a plain explanation; the workspace owner sees them for the team, along with the budget vs actual report, which compares time with the budget the owner or the task's creator set and never with anyone's own estimate; other members see only their own row of that report and the team medians. Managers and team leads see the time, flags, leave and attendance of the people they look after, never anyone's private tasks, never people the owner hid, and never Proof mode screenshots; they see rates, pay and billing only if the owner allows a manager to. With planned shifts, each person is told about a late start, a missed shift or an early finish first and can give a reason; their team lead hears only if nothing is explained in time. A client sees only billable, confirmed time on their own page, never a hidden person's name, and never notes kept inside the team.

Please do not put special categories of data, such as health details, into tasks unless your business has a lawful reason and has told the people concerned.

Proof mode screenshots

Proof mode is off unless a workspace owner switches it on for one of their clients. When it is on, it works like this:

  • What a screenshot contains. A picture of the screen, window or browser tab you choose to share, at most 1280 pixels wide, blurred so text cannot be read unless the owner chose sharp pictures for that client (the consent screen says which). It can show anything that is on that screen at that moment, which may include personal data about you or other people, such as messages, names or account details. Share a single window, close what is private, or use Pause proof for 10 minutes first. With each screenshot we keep who took it, the client, task and time entry, the time, whether it was blurred, what kind of surface was shared (entire screen, window or tab) and a small number saying how much the picture changed since the last one. We never record keystrokes, mouse clicks, the names of apps or websites, your camera or your microphone.
  • When. Only on a computer, in Chrome, Edge or Firefox, only while you share your screen in that browser and a timer or client clock runs for that client: 1 to 3 at random times in each 10 minutes, as the owner chose. Phones cannot share a screen, so nothing is captured on a phone.
  • Purpose. To give the workspace owner, and if they choose a single client, evidence that time billed to that client was spent on that work. Not for any other purpose, and never for advertising or to train AI.
  • Lawful basis. Your consent. Only you can give it, on the consent screen, for each client, and it is recorded with the time and the version of the text you read. The owner cannot consent for you. You can withdraw it at any time in your account: nothing more is captured, and screenshots the owner has not seen yet are deleted. You then cannot add time for that client until you agree again or the owner switches Proof mode off for you. The workspace owner decides to use Proof mode and is responsible for having a good reason and for telling you about it; for these screenshots they are the controller and we process them for them.
  • Who sees them. You first: each screenshot waits in your Proof tray for 10 minutes, where you can delete it. A deleted one leaves only a marker that one was removed, never the picture. After that the workspace owner can see it. Other members never see your screenshots. The client never sees them, unless the owner chooses to show a particular screenshot on a line of time on that client's page, which is recorded in the workspace history.
  • How long they are kept. 30 days by default; the owner can choose 7, 14, 30 or 60 days. Then the hourly job deletes the screenshot and its file. They are also deleted when you leave or are removed from the workspace, and with the workspace when it is deleted. Exports contain their details only, unless the owner chooses to include the pictures in a full export.
  • Where. In the same private file storage as other workspace files, under that workspace, reached only through links that last one hour.

Where data is stored and transfers

Our database is hosted in the United Kingdom and some providers above process data in the United States, the European Union and other countries. When personal data leaves Nigeria, the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom, we rely on an adequacy decision where one exists, or on the provider's standard contractual clauses and the other safeguards the NDPA and GDPR allow.

How long we keep it

  • Workspace content stays while the workspace exists. When its owner deletes a workspace, everyone loses access at once. The owner can restore it for 30 days; after that its content and files are purged from our systems.
  • Deleted tasks go to a bin for 30 days so they can be restored, then they and their files are removed.
  • Proof mode screenshots are kept for the period the workspace owner chose (7, 14, 30 or 60 days; 30 by default), then deleted with their files.
  • The history of changes in a workspace and the record of requests the app has already applied are kept for 400 days, then deleted. Copies of the emails we sent are kept for 30 days.
  • Attendance notes (whether a planned shift was late, missed or left early, when the person and their lead were told, and the reason given) and shift swap requests are kept for 400 days, then deleted. Planned shifts, roles, teams and leave balances are workspace content and are kept with it.
  • Answers to the “still working?” check are kept for 120 days, then deleted. Time entries, with their flags, claims, the owner's notes and the client's approvals and questions, are workspace content and are kept with it.
  • Usage counters used for rate limits are kept for 2 days, and monthly counts of WhatsApp messages sent are kept for 13 months.
  • When you delete your account, your sign in is removed and your name is removed from workspaces you do not own. Work you did in a workspace that belongs to someone else stays with that workspace, because it belongs to its owner.
  • Payment notices from Paystack and Lemon Squeezy are kept as a short record (type, amount, currency, reference, status and time), never the full notice, and deleted after 13 months. Invoices and the plan history on your workspace are kept for as long as tax and accounting laws require, usually 6 years in Nigeria.
  • Backups: we keep an encrypted backup of the database every night for 35 days and one a month for 13 months, and an encrypted weekly copy of attached files, stored apart from the live service with a key we keep offline. They are used only to recover the service after a fault or an accident. When a workspace is deleted, it is purged from the live service after its 30 day restore window; deleted files leave the file backups within 35 days, and the database backups that still hold it are deleted as they age out, within 13 months. If we ever have to restore a backup, we delete again anything that had already been deleted.

Your rights

Under the NDPA and the GDPR you have the right to:

  • be told how your data is used (this policy),
  • get a copy of your data. Owners can export the whole workspace at any time, each member can export everything they can see in it, and anyone can ask us for a copy of what we hold,
  • have wrong data corrected, most of which you can do yourself in your account,
  • have your data deleted. You can delete your account from your account in the app or by writing to us, and owners can delete a workspace,
  • restrict or object to some uses, including any use based on legitimate interests,
  • receive your data in a common machine readable format and have it sent to another provider,
  • withdraw consent at any time, for example by turning off location, phone alerts or WhatsApp alerts,
  • not be subject to decisions made only by automated means that have legal or similarly significant effects. We do not make such decisions,
  • complain to a regulator: the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (ndpc.gov.ng), or your local data protection authority in the EEA or the Information Commissioner's Office in the United Kingdom.

To use any of these rights, write to hello@example.com. We will answer within 30 days. We may need to confirm who you are first. If your request is about a workspace that belongs to a business, we may pass it to that business, as it controls that content.

Cookies and local storage

Handlark does not use advertising or tracking cookies, and there are no analytics trackers on the site.

The app keeps a few things in your browser's local storage so it works: your sign in session, the workspace you last opened, changes waiting to be sent when you are offline, alert settings, a referral code if you arrived through one, and on client pages the name you typed. Phones may keep a copy of the app's screens so it opens offline. Clearing your browser data removes all of this.

Cloudflare may set a strictly necessary security cookie to protect the site from abuse.

Children

Handlark is a business tool and is not meant for children. You must be at least 18 to create an account. We do not knowingly collect data from children; if you think a child has given us data, write to us and we will delete it.

How we protect data

Data travels encrypted, is stored encrypted, and each workspace is locked to its own members. Backups are encrypted before they are stored, with a key we keep offline. Our security page explains the measures in plain words. If a breach affects your personal data, we will tell the affected customers and the Nigeria Data Protection Commission within the time the law requires.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy in a way that matters, we will tell workspace owners by email or in the app before the change takes effect. The date at the top shows the latest version.
